# For cooperative and chama treasurers: the money trail your members keep asking for

**One line.** Brief is where a group keeps its pool, its members' businesses and
its collective buying in one set of rows — so "did the money move, and who is it
sitting with?" is answered by opening a screen, not by a meeting.

---

## The argument this ends

Every group has the same three recurring fights:

1. **"Has everyone contributed?"** — answered from the contribution rows.
2. **"Who is next, and who has already taken?"** — answered from the rotation.
3. **"What did we actually buy together, and did it arrive?"** — usually the one
   with no answer at all. Brief puts the group's bulk ask into the same chain any
   business uses — Request → quotes → work order → settlement — so it leaves a
   trail instead of a group-chat memory.

## What your treasurer sees (and what no one else sees)

| In Brief | Where |
|---|---|
| Pool on hand, contributed, paid out, outstanding loans — derived from recorded rows, never stored as a balance | Treasurer dashboard |
| The group's own collective requests: placed, open, quoted, accepted — with the accepted price read off the offer itself | Operator read |
| Money that actually **settled through Brief** in a stated window, in one currency or honestly refused when currencies are mixed | Operator read |
| Each member's **public** shopfront: that it exists, how many live offers, how current its own file is | Owner-only section of the operator read |
| Minutes, decisions and action items, with a PDF the group can circulate | Meetings |
| Member joins by invite code, so the roster is not a WhatsApp list someone retypes | Invites |

**What it will not show:** a member's sales with anyone outside the group; a
ranking of members by contribution; a "reliability" grade; a percentage of
"sales the group brought you". Those are not hidden — there is no row behind them,
and the screen prints the gap in words rather than a number. A member's own
business data is not readable by other members, by design and by test.

## Day one, in the order it actually happens

1. Create the group (or start from a template: merry-go-round, welfare-first).
2. Invite members by code. The roster is the group, not a chat.
3. Contributions and payouts get recorded with maker/checker — the treasurer
   cannot pay themself, and there is no stored balance to argue with.
4. Place the group's first bulk ask. Suppliers quote in Brief; the accepted
   price and the settlement sit next to the contribution rows.
5. Members put up shopfronts that say what they actually sell. That is what makes
   the group more than a pot.

## What it costs

Per group, flat, monthly. We are not quoting a number here, and we will not quote
one as a percentage of "recovered revenue" either, because Brief cannot attribute
a member's sale to the group — and a fee tied to a number we cannot compute is a
dispute waiting to happen. Say what you need priced and we will price the thing
that exists.

## What you need from us, honestly

Brief is not a bank, not a lender, not an insurer and not a registrar. It holds no
member money; M-Pesa references and receipt hashes are records you attest to, not
funds we control. If a member wants a guarantee, that is SASRA's and your
by-laws' territory.

### Bring your numbers, not ours

The figures on the screens are yours, generated the first week you use it. A
vendor or a group with no history in Brief sees zeros and one clear next action —
not a testimonial, not a benchmark, and no "average cooperative your size sees
18 views a week". There is no such table, and the dash is there instead of one.
